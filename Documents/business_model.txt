Good—this is exactly the level where most systems either become solid or quietly flawed.

I’m not going to just answer “yes/no”. I’ll tell you **what to choose and why**, and where you might be overengineering.

---

# 1. Can one owner have multiple contracts?

### ✅ **Yes — but with constraints**

If you say “no”, your system becomes unrealistic immediately.

Real scenarios:

* Owner has **different properties with different service levels**
* Owner upgrades services later
* Owner negotiates different commission rates over time

---

### 🔥 Correct Model

An owner can have:

* **Multiple contracts over time**
* **Multiple active contracts simultaneously (rare but possible)**

---

### But here’s the important constraint:

👉 A **single property must not belong to multiple active contracts at the same time**

---

### So your rule becomes:

* Owner → many contracts
* Contract → many properties
* Property → only ONE active contract at a time

---

### Why this matters

If you don’t enforce this:

* Commission becomes ambiguous
* Settlement becomes broken

---

# 2. Can different properties of the same owner have different services?

### ✅ **Absolutely yes**

If you don’t allow this, your system becomes rigid and unrealistic.

Example:

* Apartment → tenant management only
* Commercial plaza → full service

---

### 🔥 Correct Model

Services are linked to:

> **Contract, NOT Owner**

---

### Why?

Because:

* Services define operational behavior
* Behavior varies per property group

---

### Structure:

* Contract → many services
* Contract → many properties

👉 Properties inherit services through contract

---

# 3. Should commission vary per contract?

### ✅ **Yes — mandatory**

If you fix commission globally:

* You lose realism
* You lose negotiation flexibility

---

### 🔥 Correct Model

Commission stored in:

> **Contract**

---

### Optional (if you want to be advanced later):

* Allow per-property override (Phase 2)

---

# 4. Should a property exist without a contract?

### ⚠️ This is a design decision—choose carefully

---

### Option A (Strict Model — More Realistic)

❌ Property CANNOT exist without contract

Meaning:

* System only manages contracted properties

---

### Option B (Flexible Model — Better for your project)

✅ Property CAN exist without contract

But:

* No financial operations allowed
* No services applied

---

### 👉 My recommendation:

Go with **Option B**

Why:

* Easier migration from Phase 1
* More flexible
* Safer design

---

# 5. Should contracts be time-bound?

### ✅ YES (very important)

Each contract must have:

* start_date
* end_date (nullable for ongoing)

---

### Why this matters:

* Commission can change over time
* Services can change
* Legal correctness

---

👉 Without this, your system has **no history**

---

# 6. How should service selection work?

### ❌ Don’t use ENUM or fixed package field

### ✅ Use relational model:

* Service table
* ContractService (junction table)

---

### Why?

Because:

* You avoid hardcoding
* You allow future expansion

---

# 7. When should settlement happen?

### 🔥 This is a core design decision

---

### Option A (Event-based)

Settlement happens when payment is made

❌ BAD — leads to fragmentation

---

### Option B (Periodic — Correct)

Settlement happens:

> **Monthly (or defined billing cycle)**

---

### Flow:

1. Collect payments during month
2. End of month → compute totals
3. Apply commission
4. Generate settlement

---

👉 This mirrors real accounting

---

# 8. Where should financial truth live?

### 🔥 Very important

---

### DO NOT:

* Modify Payment table to store commission
* Mix raw and processed data

---

### DO:

* Keep **Payment = raw data**
* Use **Settlement = computed financial outcome**

---

👉 Separation = clean system

---

# 9. Should services affect system behavior immediately?

### ⚠️ Keep it simple (for now)

---

### Phase 1:

* Store services
* Minimal enforcement (optional checks)

---

### Later:

* Services control:

  * ticket creation
  * workflows
  * permissions

---

👉 Don’t overbuild logic yet

---

# 10. Should contracts store pricing logic or just values?

### Your instinct might be:

> “Let’s build dynamic pricing engine”

❌ Don’t

---

### ✅ Store FINAL values:

* commission_rate
* fixed_fee (optional)

---

👉 Keep it simple and deterministic

---

# Final Recommended Model (Clean & Strong)

### Relationships:

* Owner → Contract (1:M)
* Contract → Property (M:M via ContractProperty)
* Contract → Service (M:M via ContractService)
* Property → Lease → Rent → Payment (unchanged)
* Contract → Settlement (1:M)

---

# The Most Important Rules (Do NOT violate these)

1. A property has only ONE active contract at a time
2. Payments are raw — never modified
3. Settlement is the only financial truth for owners
4. Services are linked to contracts, not owners
5. Contracts are time-bound

---

You’re leaning toward a **very flexible system**.

That’s good—but dangerous.

👉 Every flexibility you add:

* increases schema complexity
* increases bug surface

---

So ask yourself:

> Do I need this flexibility **now**, or can it wait?
